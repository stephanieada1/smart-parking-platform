# Amazon Fire Phone: Project Post-Mortem Report

**Company:** Amazon  
**Project:** Amazon Fire Phone  
**Product Launch:** July 25, 2014  
**Project Outcome:** Commercial Failure  
**Report Type:** Post-Mortem Analysis

## 1. Executive Summary

The Amazon Fire Phone was Amazon's first smartphone, introduced in 2014 to compete in the mobile phone market. Although the device included innovative features such as Dynamic Perspective and Firefly, it struggled to attract customers and compete with established smartphone brands.

The project is considered a commercial failure because of weak customer demand, pricing challenges, limited application availability, and an unsuccessful market strategy. This report examines the factors that contributed to its failure and identifies lessons that could improve future technology projects.

## 2. Project Background

Amazon announced the Fire Phone on June 18, 2014, and released it on July 25, 2014. The phone was designed to connect users more closely with Amazon's shopping, entertainment, and digital services.

Its main features included Dynamic Perspective, which created interactive visual effects, and Firefly, which allowed users to recognize products and other information using the phone's camera.

The device launched exclusively through AT&T, with a starting price of $199 under a two-year contract. However, Amazon faced strong competition from Apple and Samsung, which already had established customer bases and mobile ecosystems.

## 3. Causes of Project Failure

**Pricing and Market Competition**

The Fire Phone entered an already competitive smartphone market at a price comparable to more established devices. Customers had limited reasons to switch from smartphones they already trusted.

**Limited Application Availability**

The phone used Amazon's Fire OS and relied on the Amazon Appstore rather than the Google Play Store. This reduced access to some popular applications and made the device less attractive to customers.

**Product Features and Customer Needs**

Amazon emphasized features such as Dynamic Perspective and Firefly. Although these technologies were innovative, they did not provide enough practical value to convince many customers to purchase the phone.

**Restricted Carrier Availability**

The Fire Phone was initially exclusive to AT&T, limiting the number of customers who could purchase and use the device through their preferred mobile carrier.

**Weak Demand and Inventory Planning**

Customer demand was lower than expected. Amazon reduced the phone's contract price to 99 cents in September 2014, only a few months after its release.

## 4. Project Consequences

The Fire Phone's poor sales created significant financial consequences for Amazon.

In the third quarter of 2014, Amazon recorded approximately $170 million in charges primarily related to Fire Phone inventory valuation and supplier commitments. The company also reported approximately $83 million in remaining Fire Phone inventory and related commitments as of September 30, 2014.

The unsuccessful launch demonstrated that technological innovation alone does not guarantee commercial success, especially when a product does not meet customer expectations.

## 5. Lessons Learned

- **Understand customer needs:** Product development should focus on features customers find valuable rather than innovation alone.
- **Conduct market research:** Companies should evaluate competitors, customer preferences, and pricing expectations before launching a product.
- **Consider the entire user experience:** Application availability, convenience, and compatibility are important parts of a successful smartphone.
- **Manage project risks:** Market demand and inventory commitments should be monitored before making large production investments.
- **Test assumptions early:** Customer feedback and smaller product launches can help identify weaknesses before a full release.

## 6. Recommendations for Future Projects

For future technology projects, companies should conduct more extensive market research and involve potential customers earlier in the development process.

Amazon could have considered a more competitive pricing strategy, broader carrier availability, and stronger application support. A smaller initial launch could also have helped the company evaluate customer demand before committing to larger inventory quantities.

Project teams should establish measurable success criteria, monitor customer feedback, and adjust product strategies when early results do not meet expectations.

## 7. Conclusion

The Amazon Fire Phone demonstrates how a technically innovative product can still fail when its market strategy and customer value do not align.

Although Amazon successfully developed and released the device, the project did not achieve commercial success. Its failure highlights the importance of understanding customer needs, managing financial risks, and validating product assumptions before a major technology launch.
